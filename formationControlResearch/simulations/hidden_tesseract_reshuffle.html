Hidden tesseract reshuffle

16 drones sit at the corners of a hidden 4D hypercube. Projecting it onto the sheet with rows (1,0,2,0)/√5 and (0,1,0,2)/√5 gives an exact 4×4 grid. Rotating the hidden hypercube reshuffles the drones on the sheet. Separately, the sheet bends into a cylinder (no stretching), which places the formation in 3D.

Deformable virtual structure that bends and stretches

No hypercube here. The 16 drones keep fixed slots (u, v) on a sheet, and the sheet's shape comes from a 4×4 cage of control points (a cubic Bézier surface). Moving the control points bends and stretches the sheet, and the drones follow. In auto mode the formation (3 grid spacings wide) flies through a doorway 2.2 spacings wide. It lengthens along the direction of travel and curls into a trough, then relaxes again. Squeezing straight across would crush neighbours, so the auto plan widens the fabric slightly and lets the curl do the narrowing.

Drones in their own cells: Voronoi on a bendable, squishable sheet

No fixed slots. Each drone owns the part of the sheet that is closer to it than to any other drone (its Voronoi cell), and keeps moving toward the centre of its cell (Lloyd's algorithm). Squishing the sheet reshapes the cells, so the drones redistribute by themselves; bending only lifts them up and down. Every step keeps each drone inside its buffered cell (its cell shrunk by half the safety distance), so no two drones can come closer than the safety distance on the sheet. Here the sheet narrows and lengthens for the doorway, and the formation reorganises into fewer columns instead of being crushed.

Safety distance = 0.5 grid spacings. The area rule √(2A / (√3·N)) is the spacing of a perfect hexagonal packing of N drones on area A: no arrangement can do better, so the planner may only squish while it stays above the safety distance. Lose a drone and watch the neighbours' cells grow and close the hole.
